A closer look

Eyes open. Stay alert.

A closer look at the real-time driver drowsiness detector, combining webcam analysis with phone alerts over a local hotspot.

How can a system tell a long eye closure from a normal blink?

The detector watches for several signs of drowsiness, not just a single blink. It analyzes webcam frames and can alert a paired Android phone over a local hotspot, without internet access.

  • Combines eye shape and a CNN eye-state model.
  • Tracks longer-term closure with PERCLOS.
  • Can also flag yawning and looking away.

Independent signals help make the detection more robust.

The Python and OpenCV pipeline uses Eye Aspect Ratio (EAR) alongside a compact CNN to judge eye closure. It also measures mouth opening and head pose, with calibration to adapt thresholds to the driver.

  • EAR and CNN signals can be fused.
  • Yawn and head-direction checks add context.
  • Thresholds can be calibrated for each driver.

Detect on the laptop. Alert the phone locally.

The laptop analyzes a webcam feed and sends an alert to the Android companion app over the phone’s hotspot. Try the camera demo to see face landmarks and simple eye-closure and mouth-opening cues processed in your browser.

  • Run the detector with a webcam or supported camera source.
  • Pair the phone and laptop on the same hotspot.
  • Detection and alerts do not require internet access.
